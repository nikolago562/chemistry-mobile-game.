# Make the chemistry itself the game

Learning games that became popular, rather than merely assigned, share one trait: **the thing being learned is the core verb of play, and the game checks the result, not whether the player followed the steps.** Zombie Division children played the version where division *was* the combat **7 times longer** in free time ([SHU repository](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556)). Zachtronics, Outer Wilds, Little Alchemy, Stardew Valley and Duolingo all work the same way: a small set of composable tools, a consistent system that answers back, knowledge or collections that visibly change the world, and failure that is cheap or funny. Edutainment fails in predictable ways. It alternates a fun wrapper with a block of school content ("chocolate-dipped broccoli"). It gates progress on quizzes. It repeats one template until players have learned the template and stop learning. It adds story dressing, speed pressure or monetised scarcity in place of meaningful activity. Our game's loop of lesson → demo → guided step → free step → quiz, repeated 42 times, has the first and third of these problems, which explains the "one-typed" feeling. The good news is that most of the cure uses systems the game already has: stations, collections, the exhibition room, classmates, the science fair and the in-game calendar. The highest-value changes are replacing scripted free steps and end quizzes with outcome-checked chemistry goals, backing the stations with a real (curated) reaction table, varying the lesson shape, and making the teacher react to what the player did. A ranked list of 12 recommendations closes the report.

## Popular learning games hide the lesson inside one strong verb

The most durable explanation for why some learning games are fun comes from Raph Koster: fun is the feeling of learning a pattern. Once a pattern is mastered, "the game becomes boring", and when players cannot find any pattern in the noise they get frustrated and quit ([Koster AGC 2003 keynote](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Austin-Game-Conference-2003-Keynote-Theory-of-Fun.pdf)). In this view a game does not compete with learning; good games are learning machines. Self-determination theory explains why some of them get replayed. Autonomy, competence and relatedness each independently predicted game enjoyment and future play ([Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan, Rochester](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)). Malone and Lepper's older taxonomy names the same levers from the learning side: challenge with uncertain outcomes, sensory and cognitive curiosity, control, and fantasy. They add that "the most effective fantasies are those more fully integrated with the content to be learned" ([Karl Kapp summary](https://karlkapp.com/motivation-and-games-motivational-theories-for-instructional-games/)).

The strongest experimental evidence for that integration comes from Habgood and Ainsworth's Zombie Division. In the intrinsic version, players fight skeletons by choosing a weapon that divides the skeleton's number. Compared with an extrinsic version (a quiz between levels) and a control, the intrinsic version produced more learning under fixed time limits and **7x longer voluntary play** ([SHU repository](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556); [Habgood 2007 thesis](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/10385/1/Habgood_2007_Final.pdf)). A later re-analysis argues that the mechanism is attention rather than motivation: "players only pay attention to features needed for the game task and ignore task-irrelevant information" ([Cutting & Iacovides](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/189921/1/LearningByDoingCuttingAndIacovides090822.pdf)). For a chemistry game the practical consequence is identical either way. If the player can win a moment without using the chemistry, the chemistry will be ignored.

Commercial hits outside "education" show the same pattern at scale. Zachtronics' stated method is to "create a certain minimum set of tools that create emergent properties when used together". As long as the problem stays open-ended, players find "optimizations we hadn't really dreamed of" ([Game Developer, Opus Magnum](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)). Outer Wilds removed upgrades entirely so that "knowledge [becomes] your upgrades" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)). Combination games reached enormous audiences with one loop: drag two items together, get a new item, watch a counter rise. Doodle God passed **100M downloads, 95% organic** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/after-100m-downloads-joybits-readies-an-ambitious-doodle-god-sequel-for-tablets)). Potion Craft replaced recipe lists with an explorable map in which each ingredient pushes a bottle in a direction, and sold **100k copies in three days** ([Gamespress](https://gamespress.com/Potion-Craft-sells-100k-copies-in-the-first-three-days); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/potion-craft-has-finally-given-me-a-crafting-system-i-dont-hate)). Cozy life-sims teach their own economies and social systems through readable, incremental progress. Stardew Valley passed **41M copies** by the end of 2024 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/stardew-valley-sales-grew-to-over-41-million-by-2024-s-end)), and Animal Crossing: New Horizons reached **50.29M** ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/150276/animal-crossing-new-horizons-50-million-units-sold)).

Even the collapse of 1990s edutainment supports this reading. The Cooney Center concluded that the decline was driven by business and market factors (the Mattel/Learning Company deal, cost-cutting, the collapse of retail software), not by a lack of demand or an inability to make good products ([Cooney Center](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=7052)). The titles remembered fondly, such as Zoombinis, are the ones where the learning was the play. Research on Zoombinis found that gameplay builds implicit computational thinking that play logs can detect, and that teachers then "bridge" it to explicit knowledge ([TERC](https://www.terc.edu/?p=2136); [Baker lab paper](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/ZoombinisHLM.pdf)).

## Ten mechanics that make learning feel like play

### Let the player try first, then name what they saw

Brilliant poses a problem or manipulation before giving an explanation, so the first attempt gives the explanation a purpose ([Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/how-brilliant-teaches-math/)). The research behind this is "productive failure". Sinha and Kapur's meta-analysis of 53 studies found that problem solving followed by instruction beats the reverse order, with **g = 0.36**, and more for high-fidelity designs ([ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/087241d3-ca0e-469e-a7f3-df9c122e2585)). There is an important caveat for our audience: the benefit holds for secondary and older learners, while grades 2–5 trended the other way and favoured instruction first. Nintendo's level design reaches the same result without words. A mechanic is introduced safely, developed, twisted and resolved (kishōtenketsu), so the level teaches instead of a lecture ([Wikipedia: Koichi Hayashida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koichi_Hayashida)). PhET's chemistry sims "guide without feeling guided" through affordances, constraints, cueing and feedback. In one study, 80 general chemistry students learned from a sim with no instructions at all ([RSC CERP 2013](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2013/rp/c3rp20157k); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.6544)).

### Check the outcome and accept any valid route

Every strong systems game validates the result and leaves the path free. Opus Magnum accepts any machine that produces the product. Obra Dinn confirms deductions only in groups of three, which stops brute-forcing and still makes the silence informative ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83249)). Ace Attorney's first prototype, which required catching contradictions in real time, "wasn't fun". The game became fun once it simply waited until the player presented the right evidence, so players felt they had solved the case through their own reasoning ([Giant Bomb, Takumi interview](https://www.giantbomb.com/profile/tansuikabutsu/blog/shu-takumi-part-4/78216/)). Baba Is You went further and kept tester-found alternative solutions as bonus "variant" levels instead of patching them out ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-baba-is-you-i-s-delightfully-innovative-rule-writing-system)).

### Use a real rule system the player can poke at

Discovery only works when a consistent system sits under the surface. Collisions, a set of eight chemistry games, is explicitly "grounded in the rules of chemistry" across 50+ concepts ([ChemEd X](https://chemedx.org/node/4721)). Minecraft Education's Chemistry Update turned its stations into collections: an Element Constructor built from proton, neutron and electron counts, a Compound Creator, a Lab Table and a Material Reducer ([Minecraft Education blog](https://education.minecraft.net/blog/introducing-chemistry-update-for-minecraft-education-edition)). The most common complaint about the realistic iPad lab CHEMIST is breadth, not graphics: "barely any reactions outside of metal and acids", with no control over concentration ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/chemist-by-thix/id440666387)). Little Alchemy shows the downside of the opposite approach. Its combination logic is associative, not chemical, so collecting there teaches little ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/little-alchemy)). A chemistry game can keep the addictive loop and make the rules real.

### Show "?" slots in a collection of known size

Completion pressure comes from visible gaps and a "found X of Y" counter (Little Alchemy, Doodle God). Surprise comes from unexpected outputs, and Infinite Craft's escalation to "God" and "Yin Yoda" is part of its appeal ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/this-browser-based-endless-crafting-game-starts-you-off-with-fire-and-water-but-it-quickly-escalates-to-god-the-big-bang-and-yin-yoda)). Status comes from Infinite Craft's global "First Discovery" tag ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Craft)). Collections become *meaningful* when they build something visible. Completing Stardew's Community Center bundles restores rooms and unlocks map areas ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/stardew-valley-dev-shares-easily-missed-but-extremely-useful-community-center-tip)). In Animal Crossing, each museum donation comes with Blathers' commentary and joins a themed wing, so it feels "part of a whole" ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Blathers); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/i-love-animal-crossing-new-horizons-museum-1842798339)).

### Make failure funny, readable and free

Kerbal Space Program grew out of its creator dismantling fireworks as a child ("We lost a lot of kerbals in those days"), and it treats explosions as comedy and story ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/kerbal_space_program/b/pc/archive/2013/09/01/the-story-of-kerbal-space-program)). Consequence-free spectacle is a huge draw on its own: Melon Sandbox reports **150M+ downloads and a 40-minute average session** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/melon-sandbox-150m-installs-and-ugc-evolution/)). The working formula is an instant retry, a slightly absurd visual, a cause the player can read, and nothing permanent lost.

### Give self-competition several axes and no single best answer

Opus Magnum scores every solution on cost, cycles and area, then shows a histogram of all players instead of a ranking. "Solved" becomes "solved, but could be better on X" without any in-game reward for optimising ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/perfectly-solving-opus-magnums-puzzles-is-impossible-but-thats-ok)). A histogram cannot humiliate anyone. It simply shows that better solutions exist.

### Space and mix the practice so it doesn't feel like review

Dunlosky's review rates practice testing (retrieval) and spaced practice as the two highest-utility study techniques, and interleaving as moderate ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)). Duolingo's half-life-regression scheduler, which decides when to bring back each item, raised daily engagement by **12%** ([ACL P16-1174](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174)). The right review item at the right time works as an engagement lever, not only a learning one. Meta-analysis also shows multi-session games working far better than single sessions (**g = 0.44 vs 0.08**) ([Clark et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4748544)). That favours a 42-lesson game, provided earlier ideas keep coming back.

### Turn knowledge into progression and mystery

Outer Wilds gates its world by understanding, with clues on one planet pointing to the next mystery. Its team learned that a dramatic event does not land until players know the place: a meteor crashing into an unfamiliar village fell flat ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)). Real classrooms show the effect for chemistry. A Clue-style forensic case significantly raised high-school students' interest in chemistry, with the largest gains among students who liked it least ([CERP 2018](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/rp/c7rp00232g)). The RSC uses flame tests and pH tests on "evidence" with Year 7 pupils ([RSC Education](https://edu.rsc.org/ideas/how-to-solve-a-murder/4014835.article)). These are mostly single-cohort self-report studies, so the evidence for interest is stronger than the evidence for lasting learning.

### Let relationships and time budgets give a reason to come back

Stardew's friendship system is readable: 250 points per heart, loved gifts worth +80, birthdays worth 8x, heart events at thresholds, and a gift log that turns learning someone's tastes into information-gathering ([Stardew Wiki](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/Friendship)). Persona's Hashino says that struggling to use limited school time well is what makes the series feel like reliving high school ([Inverse](https://nc.inverse.com/gaming/katsura-hashino-game-changers-persona-metaphor)). Potionomics ties friendship to mechanical growth: each friend contributes new cards to the player's deck ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/how-potionomics-turned-price-haggling-into-a-card-game)).

### Have a character who praises effort

Duolingo's in-lesson coach, using growth-mindset copy that praises effort and improvement, beat both "you're amazing" copy and no coach, with **+7.2% D14 retention**. A personal "Hi, it's Duo" notification raised DAU by 5% ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker/)). In learning-game meta-analysis, teacher-provided scaffolding was the strongest moderator found (**g = 0.58**) ([Clark et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4748544)).

## Edutainment fails when the game and the lesson take turns

The classic failure has a name. Amy Bruckman's "chocolate-dipped broccoli" describes software that sweetens content with unrelated fun, with Math Blaster as her main example ([Tedium](https://tedium.co/2019/05/09/edutainment-math-blaster-chocolate-covered-broccoli)). The phrase came up in every GDC 1999 education roundtable ([Ken Kahn](https://www.toontalk.com/English/easydoit.htm)). Being a game is not enough on its own. Wouters et al. found serious games were **not significantly more motivating** than conventional instruction (d = 0.26, n.s.), even though they produced modest learning gains (d = 0.29) ([Utrecht](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-cognitive-and-motivational-effects-of-seri/)).

The meta-analytic data hold an uncomfortable warning for anyone who thinks more story or more spectacle will cure boredom. Clark et al. found that **more contextual dressing correlated with smaller learning effects** (slope −0.07, p = .01). Realistic visuals scored g = −0.01 against 0.48 for schematic visuals, though that gap became marginal after controls. Single-player non-competitive play scored 0.45, against −0.06 for competitive play ([Clark et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4748544)). The same paper found no significant difference between categories of intrinsic integration, which conflicts somewhat with Habgood. The fairest reading is that Habgood's controlled manipulation is stronger evidence than Clark's post-hoc coding of mixed studies. Treat intrinsic integration as well supported for engagement and plausible for learning. Either way, the failure is in the activity structure, not a shortage of fantasy.

Beyond the broccoli problem, the failure modes in the notes fall into three families. The first is **structural**. Players master a repeated template, and from then on only the content changes (Koster). Quiz-gating turns failure into a wall instead of information, which works against SDT's competence need; no controlled study isolates this, so it is an inference from theory. Hidden, deep prerequisite chains block players, as reviewers said of Graveyard Keeper ([Bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/graveyard-keeper-review/1)). Crafting with no outlet feels pointless, which is why Potion Craft's map was criticised for being poorly connected to its customers ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/potion-craft-alchemist-simulator-relaxing-alchemy-shop-sim-impressions/)). Too much opacity makes discovery impossible: Cultist Simulator's early versions were "so opaque as to be almost unplayable" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-experimental-game-i-cultist-simulator-i-for-commercial-success)).

The second family is **pressure and reward design**. Kahoot studies report stress, guessing and "fear of losing", and in one study **24%** of students found the live leaderboard stressful ([Wang & Tahir 2020](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/39435); [CDHA](https://files.cdha.ca/profession/journal/2841.pdf)). Leaderboards built on raw XP get farmed with easy repeats ([MetaFilter](https://www.metafilter.com/199369/I-Am-Going-Out-In-A-Blaze-Of-Glory)). Purely cosmetic gamification does nothing: Duolingo's first "moves counter" was "completely neutral" for retention ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Manipulative scarcity draws backlash. Duolingo's switch from hearts to an energy meter that drains even on correct answers drew petitions ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/)), and an FTC complaint against Prodigy counted **16 membership ads against 4 math problems** in one session ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02)).

The third family is **mistaking game success for learning**. DragonBox players reached high in-game mastery (**92.9%** within 1.5 hours) ([DragonBox](https://dragonbox.com/about/algebra-challenge)), but in one study they gained no more than students using a tutoring system ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8453p0gf/qt8453p0gf.pdf?t=sgiu9f)). Overclaiming is the commercial version of the same error: Lumosity paid $2M to settle FTC charges of unsupported cognitive-benefit claims ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)).

Duolingo's leagues complicate the anti-competition evidence. They raised learning time by **17%** and **tripled** the number of highly engaged learners ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). The way to reconcile this with Clark is that competition drives *time on task*, while head-to-head competition *during* the learning activity hurts learning. For a cozy game, the safe synthesis is competition you cannot lose: histograms, multiple ribbon categories, personal bests, promotion without demotion, and scoring that rewards new mastery rather than repetition.

## Our loop teaches the template, then stops teaching

Seen through this evidence, the game's problem is structural rather than a lack of content. All 42 lessons share one five-beat shape. Players will master that shape within the first chapter, and Koster predicts boredom after that point even if the reactions change. The end-of-lesson quiz is the textbook extrinsic segment: the chemistry happens at the bench, then gets checked in a different, non-game format. The teacher reveals each concept before the player meets it, which removes Malone's curiosity lever. The guided and free steps follow a set order, which removes control and autonomy. Each free step tests only the concept just taught, which removes the "which idea applies here?" judgement that interleaving research values. Every lesson is equally learning-dense, so the life-sim's cozy downtime never contrasts with it.

The game's assets are strong, and most of them are currently underused rather than missing. Stations can become a rule-driven bench. Collections can become a "?"-slot substance book. The exhibition room can become a museum with commentary. Storage, the reading room and the greenhouse can become bundle targets. Seven classmates can become relationship arcs and spaced-retrieval "customers". The science fair can become the multi-category showcase and judging round. The teacher NPC, who already walks, swirls and pours at her desk, can become the reactive coach that the strongest meta-analytic moderator describes. Clark's finding that single-player, non-competitive, multi-session play performs best also fits the game's format.

### 1. Replace the scripted free step and end quiz with outcome-checked goals

**Sources and why it works.** Zachtronics checks only the product and accepts any machine ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)). Zombie Division produced more learning and 7x the voluntary play by making the content the action ([SHU](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556)). PhET shows that students learn from a well-constrained sim without prior instruction ([RSC CERP](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2013/rp/c3rp20157k)).

**In our game.** The free step stops being "add 5 ml HCl now" and becomes a goal stated in-world: "Make the greenhouse soil less acidic", or "Make something that turns the indicator purple", or "Produce a gas that clouds the limewater". The station validates the end state (a pH range, a colour, a product) and accepts any valid route, for example neutralising with NaOH or with Ca(OH)₂. Most lesson-end quizzes become silent logs of what the player actually produced. Keep one short, plain-symbol "notebook" question per chapter as a transfer check, because DragonBox shows that in-game mastery does not guarantee test performance.

### 2. Back every station with a curated reaction table and add a free-mix bench

**Sources and why it works.** Collisions and PhET work because a consistent rule system sits underneath. CHEMIST users complain about narrow reaction coverage, not about graphics ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/chemist-by-thix/id440666387)). Little Alchemy and Doodle God prove that the drag-two-things loop is compulsive ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/after-100m-downloads-joybits-readies-an-ambitious-doodle-god-sequel-for-tablets)).

**In our game.** Hand-author a ScriptableObject table of reagent pairs → product, visual (colour, bubbles, precipitate), Georgian equation and a "simplified" flag. Every unlisted pair returns a sensible "no visible reaction" plus a reason category (Cu + HCl is itself a lesson). Do not use an LLM; correctness and safety need curation. Recommendation 1 needs this table, and so do the free-mix bench in the personal lab and the substance book. Reveal notation gradually, following DragonBox ([DragonBox](https://dragonbox.com/about/algebra-challenge)): coloured atom tokens first, then Georgian names, then symbols, then balanced equations as chapters progress. Mark dangerous combinations such as bleach + acid as teacher-blocked, with a one-line explanation.

### 3. Make the teacher react to what the player did

**Sources and why it works.** Growth-mindset coach copy gave Duolingo +7.2% D14 retention ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker/)). Teacher-provided scaffolding was the largest moderator in Clark et al. (g = 0.58) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4748544)).

**In our game.** Write short, rotating Georgian barks keyed to logged events: the player checked the colour twice, used an unexpected reagent, failed and retried, or found a new route ("Oh, I never thought of it that way!"). Praise the method, not talent. When the player loads a save, the teacher gives a warm, guilt-free nudge ("I saved your seat; the greenhouse needs its pH test"). This is mostly dialogue content on top of the existing TeacherNPC, which makes it the cheapest high-impact change on the list.

### 4. Break the single lesson template with try-first openers and twists

**Sources and why it works.** Productive failure outperforms instruction-first for secondary learners (g = 0.36) ([ETH](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/087241d3-ca0e-469e-a7f3-df9c122e2585)). Nintendo's introduce → develop → twist → resolve structure teaches without a lecture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koichi_Hayashida)). Brilliant asks before it tells ([Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/how-brilliant-teaches-math/)).

**In our game.** Author four or five lesson shapes and rotate them across the 42 lessons. Keep the current **teacher-first** shape for hard or abstract topics and younger players. Add a **mystery bench** shape (two unlabelled flasks, free mixing, then the teacher explains "you saw it turn pink, here's why"), a **classmate problem** shape (a stained shirt or a cloudy aquarium the player tries to fix before the lesson), an **identify the unknown** shape, and a **limited resources** or **fix a broken process** shape. Within each chapter, run Ki–Shō–Ten–Ketsu. Fizz chalk with acid. Vary the carbonates. Then the twist: this "chalk" does not fizz, so what is it? Resolve it in a mission. Fade the guided step across a chapter instead of giving every lesson the same amount of help, and alternate dense lessons with cozy downtime. Because the meta-analysis reverses for grades 2–5, tag lessons by target age and default to teacher-first for younger players.

### 5. Turn mistakes into funny, readable lab mishaps

**Sources and why it works.** KSP made failure the joke and the story ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/kerbal_space_program/b/pc/archive/2013/09/01/the-story-of-kerbal-space-program)). Melon Sandbox shows the reach of consequence-free cause and effect ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/melon-sandbox-150m-installs-and-ugc-evolution/)).

**In our game.** A wrong mix triggers a cozy mishap: foam overflow, a coloured smoke puff, a soot-faced sprite, or a cat leaping off a shelf. Each comes with a one-line Georgian "what happened" card (e.g., too much bicarbonate, so CO₂ was released). Mishaps model the correct safety response (goggles, fume hood, the teacher's fire blanket) so the humour never teaches recklessness. Add a "Mishaps" page to the collections so failing becomes something to collect, and give every bench a one-tap reset and refill. This reuses the reaction table's "wrong" outcomes.

### 6. Build a "?"-slot substance book and a museum that talks back

**Sources and why it works.** Known-size collections with visible gaps drive completion (Little Alchemy, Doodle God). Blathers' commentary makes each Animal Crossing donation a small lesson delivered as a reward ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Blathers)). Minecraft Education turned element and compound creation into a collection ([Minecraft Education](https://education.minecraft.net/blog/introducing-chemistry-update-for-minecraft-education-edition)).

**In our game.** The substance book shows silhouettes with a category and a hint ("burns with a lilac flame"), a counter ("23/60 compounds"), and three tiers per entry: seen in a demo, made yourself, tested. Completing a row (for example all alkali-metal chlorides) unlocks a bonus scene with the teacher. Each first-time product can be donated to the exhibition room, where the teacher or a curator classmate gives two or three lines in Georgian: what it is, where it appears in daily life, and one fun fact. The commentary *is* the lesson, delivered after the discovery instead of before it. A "first in your class" tag, with classmates reacting, gives Infinite Craft's status reward offline.

### 7. Let bundles visibly restore the school

**Sources and why it works.** Stardew's bundles give collections a purpose: completing a room's set restores the room and unlocks new areas. A small pulsing icon flags inventory items a bundle still needs ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/stardew-valley-dev-shares-easily-missed-but-extremely-useful-community-center-tip); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-creator-shares-a-game-changing-tip-hardly-anyone-noticed)).

**In our game.** Start storage, the reading room and the greenhouse partly run-down: a dusty shelf, broken irrigation, an empty display. Each needs a themed bundle, such as three acids, two indicators and one grown salt crystal. Completing it physically repairs the room and unlocks a station or mission. Add the pulse icon to the inventory. Bundles group naturally by chemical family, so completing them reinforces classification.

### 8. Use classmate requests as spaced, interleaved retrieval

**Sources and why it works.** Retrieval and spacing are the highest-utility learning techniques, and interleaving adds the "which tool applies?" judgement ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)). Duolingo's scheduler raised engagement by 12% ([ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174)). Potionomics and Moonlighter give crafting a second system to feed into ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/how-potionomics-turned-price-haggling-into-a-card-game); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/moonlighter-review)).

**In our game.** Keep a simple Leitner box per concept: a correct answer doubles the interval, a wrong one resets it. When a concept comes due, it reappears as an in-world request mixed with other concept types. A classmate needs a pH-7 solution in chapter 6, the canteen has a soap problem, or the greenhouse plants are yellowing. Fulfilling a request earns friendship, decorations or bundle items. Players practise retrieval without ever seeing a "review" screen, and the personal lab gains a reason to produce things.

### 9. Score experiments on several axes and judge the science fair by ribbon

**Sources and why it works.** Opus Magnum's cost, cycles and area histograms create replay without rewards or humiliation ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)). Ace Attorney's waiting "present the contradiction" moment makes the player's own reasoning the payoff ([Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/profile/tansuikabutsu/blog/shu-takumi-part-4/78216/)). Duolingo shows that weekly cohort resets drive time on task ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)).

**In our game.** After a free step or research project, show two or three metrics with chemical meaning: reagent waste, steps or time, and purity or safety. Display them against a pre-baked histogram of classmates' results, or against the player's own past attempts, and attach no progress gate. The weekly science fair awards several ribbons (most efficient, purest, most creative method), so different play styles win. It ends with a club-tier promotion that never demotes, and league points come only from new mastery, so repeating easy tasks earns nothing. Add one judging round where a classmate claims "this powder is salt" and the player presents a notebook result (it fizzed with acid). The round has no timer. Any quiz that remains should weight correctness over speed and offer a calm, untimed mode, in line with the Kahoot evidence.

### 10. Give classmates hearts, chemistry passions and a time budget

**Sources and why it works.** Stardew's readable hearts and heart events ([Stardew Wiki](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/Friendship)), Persona's scarce school time ([Inverse](https://nc.inverse.com/gaming/katsura-hashino-game-changers-persona-metaphor)), Potionomics' friend-granted abilities, and Spiritfarer's farewells, which are earned through familiarity ([Vice](https://creators.vice.com/en_us/article/n7wywg/spiritfarer-a-game-of-hugging-and-letting-go-is-perfect-for-right-now)).

**In our game.** Split each school day into periods (lesson, break, after school, evening lab), with each free slot spent on one thing: a classmate, a research project, greenhouse care or the personal lab. The existing bell marks period ends and natural save points. Each of the seven classmates gets a friendship meter raised by talking, helping at their station, and gifts the player *made* (a grown crystal, an indicator dye). Preferences are learned from other NPCs and recorded in a gift log, and there is no decay, to keep the tone cozy. Each heart threshold opens a door into one area of chemistry. The perfume-loving classmate asks for help with distillation, and the reward is a station upgrade or a better indicator on loan. A gentle term-end farewell (a classmate leaving for an olympiad) pays off what the player learned about them.

### 11. Run a term-long school mystery with a lab-notebook knowledge web

**Sources and why it works.** Outer Wilds makes knowledge the only progression and holds big events until the world is familiar ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)). Obra Dinn's checks in threes stop guessing without punishment ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83249)). Forensic-chemistry cases raised interest most among the least motivated students ([CERP 2018](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/rp/c7rp00232g)).

**In our game.** Seed a kid-safe whodunit only after the player knows the hallway, storage and greenhouse well. Who keeps changing the colour of the greenhouse flowers? What turned the exhibition's display liquid cloudy? What is the teacher working on at her desk? Each learned fact becomes a node in the lab notebook, linked to open questions, and new rooms and missions unlock by what the player knows. Flame-test, pH, precipitation and chromatography results count as evidence. The mystery board confirms deductions only when three are correct together, with a distinct jingle on the "click". The reveal recasts the setting: the "magic" flowers are a natural pH indicator. This is the highest-effort item because it needs writing, art and state tracking across the term. It builds on items 2, 6 and 8, so it belongs late in the schedule.

### 12. Adopt guardrails that protect the cozy tone

**Sources and why it works.** Duolingo's Energy backlash ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/)), Kahoot time-pressure anxiety ([Wang & Tahir](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/39435)), Prodigy's status gaps ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02)), Lumosity's overclaims ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)), Graveyard Keeper's opaque chains ([Bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/graveyard-keeper-review/1)), Cultist Simulator's opacity, and Clark's finding that more dressing correlates with less learning.

**In our game.** Never add a meter that drains on correct play, and never make mistakes cost anything beyond a little time and a funny animation. Speed alone never scores. There is no demotion and no guilt messaging. Keep research-project recipe trees visible in the notebook and at most two or three steps deep at a time. Every Cultist-style unlabelled bottle in storage gets a fallback hint ladder (category → one ingredient → full recipe), following Layton's hint coins found around the school ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Layton_and_the_Curious_Village)). Keep decoration and ambient effects quiet during learning beats, because attention goes only to what the task needs. On the store page, describe the curriculum topics covered and do not promise better grades.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we make lessons more fun?" to "how do we stop separating the lesson from the play?" The evidence that more story, more realism or more competition improves learning games is weak or negative. The evidence that integrating content into the core verb, checking outcomes and spacing retrieval improves them is the strongest the field has. For this game that means the biggest win is not a new system but a change in what the existing stations ask of the player. The current loop asks the player to follow. The recommended loop asks the player to produce, predict, identify or fix something, and lets the teacher, the classmates and the school respond.

There is a sequencing implication too. Items 1–3 (outcome goals, a reaction table, a reactive teacher) are the foundation the rest depend on: collections, bundles, requests, scoring and the mystery all consume reaction-table outcomes and logged player actions. Building that layer first turns the 42 lessons from a fixed sequence into content for a system. The open uncertainty is the audience's age. Productive failure and opaque discovery help teenagers but can hurt younger children, so the try-first and mystery features should be playtested with the actual target grade before they replace teacher-first lessons wholesale.
